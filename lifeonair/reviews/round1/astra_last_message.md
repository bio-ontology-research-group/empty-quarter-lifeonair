**Part 1 — Review**

The manuscript titled “Plant roots and long-term aridity set the atmospheric hydrogen-scavenging capacity of hyperarid Rub’ al-Khali soils” combines a 5,438-genome catalogue with read-based functional profiling of 550 metagenomes. It reports a pronounced root-associated reduction in group-1l hydrogenase abundance, contrasting distributions of hydrogen and carbon monoxide oxidation genes, and an association between group-1l abundance and long-term precipitation. Vegetation indices and predicted growth traits are used to investigate possible mechanisms. The strongest contribution is the replicated, depth-matched group-1l contrast. However, an inverted CoxL classification rule compromises the CO results, and the temporal analysis covers substantially less time than the manuscript implies.

I recommend **major revision**. The root-associated group-1l pattern appears recoverable, but the manuscript’s present three-hypothesis narrative is not supported. H2 requires reconstruction after correcting CoxL classification. H3 must distinguish an association with spatial precipitation from evidence about temporal responses or climate-change substitution. Several mechanistic statements exceed what these observational, relative-abundance data establish.

The audit reproduced the original read aggregation to its reported precision: maximum GE discrepancy was below \(5\times10^{-6}\). All 573 archived screening metadata records specified 100-nt truncation and a 25-aa minimum alignment; the archive and local database tables matched their recorded checksums. I also reproduced the principal group-1l contrast and spatial correlation. These checks establish numerical traceability, not biological validity of the labels. I did not rerun DIAMOND, tree inference, or the complete R workflows. No files were modified. The PDF integrity scan detected no reviewer-directed instructions; its manuscript flags concerned small figure text.

Major issues follow.

**Marker identity must be corrected before interpreting the ecology (R1-01, R1-02).** The CoxL classifier reverses the diagnostic motifs. `AYRGAGR` is associated with form II, whereas the characteristic form-I motif is `AYXCSFR`. This distinction is explicit in primary sequence analyses. [Martin-Cuadrado et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2786428/) The error is consequential: 1,627 of 1,901 reported form-I proteins were admitted solely by `AYRGAGR`, without passing the identity/coverage criterion.

I performed a diagnostic relabelling in memory, retaining the identity/coverage criterion and recognising the form-I motif, without changing alignments. This reduced the candidate form-I set to 285 proteins. Approximately 90.1% of reads originally assigned to form I across the screened libraries were reassigned. Bulk-soil median form-I GE changed from 0.616 to 0.105. The surface/bulk contrast changed from −0.020 to −0.502 log₂ units, with site-level Wilcoxon \(p=0.00106\). The root/bulk contrast became −0.344, \(p=0.0284\). These are diagnostic recalculations, not a substitute for curated reclassification, but they invalidate the current claim of position-independent CO capacity. The group-1l root contrast remained approximately −2.23, and its excess remained approximately −1.97 under this correction.

The hydrogenase control also needs revision. Group 2a includes experimentally demonstrated atmospheric H₂ oxidisers, yet all group-2a reads enter the supposedly non-high-affinity control. [Islam et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7784904/) The dataset contains 140 group-2a proteins, and their reads account for 24.1% of the primary cohort’s other-[NiFe] control reads. Moreover, Grinter et al., cited for the introductory enzyme statement, characterise Huc, a group-2a enzyme. Restricting the focal comparison to groups 1h and 1l is legitimate; defining them as the complete atmospheric-H₂ guild and placing group 2a in a negative control is not.

**The temporal test is not a five-expedition, 31-month test (R1-03, R1-04, R1-11).** All expedition-5 antecedent precipitation values are missing because the climate record ends in 2024. The bulk-soil temporal analysis contains 71 visits at 27 sites between March 2023 and August 2024, approximately 17 months. Fourteen sites contribute only two visits. This exclusion must be explicit in the abstract, methods, results, and interpretation, or the precipitation record must be extended.

Comparing a 30-year spatial climate normal with preceding-year rainfall does not directly test the response to a sustained change in aridity. Nor does a nonsignificant temporal coefficient establish independence from rain. The manuscript also compares the temporal confidence interval with a spatial point estimate rather than estimating their difference. My joint site bootstrap retained a positive temporal-minus-spatial difference, approximately 1.05–6.31, so propagating spatial uncertainty alone does not eliminate that contrast. However, this still compares different environmental quantities and treats sites as independent despite only four shared expeditions. The defensible conclusion is a difference between the sampled spatial and short-term associations, subject to the design’s limitations.

Within-site centring does not make visit residuals independent. Their ordinary Spearman \(p\)-values remain inappropriate for repeated observations. Conversely, the trip-adjusted sample-level mixed model uses uncentred precipitation, mixing within-site and between-site information. These analyses should be replaced by an explicit within/between decomposition with the relevant replication structure.

**The hypotheses are exploratory, and the primary cohort is inconsistently defined (R1-05, R1-06).** The results README states that the original hypotheses were contradicted and replacement hypotheses were developed after reassessing the same data. Repeated statements that results occurred “as H1/H3 predicted” therefore misrepresent the inferential sequence. Report this as exploratory hypothesis development and distinguish any genuinely independent validation.

The 61-location analysis includes four samples from sites 62 and 64. The companion data descriptor identifies these as special-interest locations outside the repeated 1–60 transect; the site-alias table identifies a road location and a campground. Restricting to sites 1–60 gives 546 samples at 59 sites and 106 root/bulk visits at 47 sites. The group-1l contrast remains strong, approximately −2.275 log₂, but the manuscript needs a consistent sampling frame and a documented inclusion ledger.

**Relative depletion does not establish loss of organisms or selection against the enzyme (R1-07, R1-08, R1-09).** GE is a relative marker-copy measure, and the lineage analyses are also compositional. The available DNA records make biomass differences a concrete concern: after excluding conflicting/nonpositive concentration records, 48 paired site visits had a median root/bulk DNA-concentration ratio of approximately 9.78. This is not a valid absolute microbial-biomass correction, because extraction and plant DNA can contribute, but it demonstrates why lower copies per genome cannot be equated with fewer carriers per gram.

Matching depth removes one confounder. It does not isolate plant carbon from moisture, oxygen, soil properties, plant identity, or microsite selection. The statements that plants “select against” group 1l and that aridity “sets” its abundance require stronger evidence or associative wording. Likewise, attenuation after adjustment for composition PC1 does not demonstrate mediation through lineage composition. It is compatible with mediation, confounding, and adjustment for a variable partly constructed from the same carrier abundances.

**The mechanism analyses do not quantify the contribution of plant carbon (R1-10, R1-22).** A 250-m NDVI measurement and predicted maximal growth potential are imperfect proxies for carbon availability at a root microsite. A residual intercept of −1.59 does not estimate the fraction of the effect unexplained by carbon, particularly without measurement-error treatment or a mediation model. “Plant-carbon proxies explain a minor part” should be replaced by a narrower statement about the observed associations.

The genus analysis also needs stronger treatment of phylogeny, prevalence, and uncertainty. It treats 288 genus summaries as observations, but 123 have exactly zero median fold change and 31 genera have only one representative contributing to their traits. The response, growth predictor, and carriage predictors all depend on the recovered MAG catalogue, with incomplete genomes treated as non-carriers when a marker is absent. Phylum adjustment cannot establish an enzyme-specific effect beyond lineage ecology.

**Several statistical analyses do not respect the experimental structure (R1-12–R1-18).** The main paired H1 analysis appropriately resamples sites. This protection is not carried consistently into the NDVI, H2, H3, and mechanism analyses, where co-located samples share environmental values but several models omit a site-visit effect. The spatial bootstrap over six blocks is useful sensitivity evidence, but needs residual spatial diagnostics and sensitivity to block definition. Independent climate products support the spatial association; they do not validate the temporal rainfall exposure.

The PERMANOVA uses unrestricted permutations, although samples are nested within visits and repeatedly collected at sites. Its 29.4% site fraction and 2.0% expedition fraction also correspond to very different numbers of factor degrees of freedom. Their ratio is not evidence that lineages persisted or that ecological integration takes longer than 31 months.

The synchrony calculation pools positions within each visit despite strong position effects and uneven sampling. Changes in compartment representation can therefore generate synchrony. More generally, synchrony measures covariation, not persistence.

Even before correcting CoxL, its compartment LRT was significant, \(p=4.87\times10^{-6}\). A changing H₂/CO ratio demonstrates different proportional responses, not independent regulation or equivalence of CO abundance across positions. Finally, secondary tests require multiplicity control. For example, the highlighted surface-CoxL temporal \(p=0.00983\) becomes approximately 0.0738 under BH adjustment across just the nine trip-adjusted marker-by-position tests.

**Sequence provenance and measurement validity need further work (R1-19–R1-21, R1-23, R1-24).** The `_sub` annotation resolvers can retrieve parent-bin annotations without restricting them to retained contigs. Two representatives explicitly removed from the official hydrogenase guild still contribute three group-1l proteins to the read database. The growth workflow also processes 1,010 `_sub` representatives through this resolver. The catalogue census, trait analyses, and sequence database therefore need reconciliation at the retained-contig level.

Competitive matching within this targeted database does not establish sensitivity and specificity against broader homologous families. The marker and ribosomal reference sets also have different coverage and clustering procedures. Benchmark the short-read classifier and GE calibration, particularly across the taxonomic compositions being compared.

The assertion that the shared denominator cancels exactly is false for the implemented pseudocount-adjusted ratios of replicate medians. Without a pseudocount, the paired group-1l median is −2.529 rather than −2.231; no paired cell requires a pseudocount for that contrast. The qualitative pattern survives, but the headline magnitude and invariance argument require qualification.

Contamination screening is incomplete across batches, and the nine available PMA pairs are not analysed. These limitations matter particularly for claims about persistence and root-associated loss in low-biomass soil.

**The literature supports a narrower novelty claim and a less directional moisture mechanism (R1-25, R1-26).** Li et al. already combine desert shrub contrasts, carbon measurements/additions, trace-gas uptake, and life-history traits. López et al. directly establish predicted growth potential as a major correlate of rhizosphere enrichment. The contribution here should centre on the replicated group-resolved association in this transect, after classification correction. Li et al. also performed hydrogenase subgroup typing; their reported groups require explicit comparison rather than characterising their analysis simply as one undifferentiated hydrogen function.

Jordaan et al. report relative declines in group-1l genes/transcripts after hydration, but also strongly stimulated H₂ uptake and explicitly caution about relative expression. Imminger et al. report increased group-1l transcription during hydration. These studies do not support a general rule that wetter root-adjacent soil suppresses group-1l physiology. Stable DNA composition can coexist with rapid metabolic responses.

**Reproducibility remains incomplete despite the useful frozen outputs (R1-27, R1-28).** The archived counts and checksums are valuable. However, the advertised fetch route requires KAUST credentials, upstream typing is outside the supplied analysis-code directory, several inputs use private absolute paths, and P3 claims are absent from the repository’s claim-verification harness. The sensitivity code also does not rerun all H1–H3 analyses as the methods claim: it omits the temporal analyses and the primary H2 raw-read-ratio contrast.

Minor issues follow.

The operon screen establishes neighbourhood co-occurrence, not complete operons: it does not require the reported gene order or strand consistency (R1-29). Figures need larger text at final manuscript size, annotated phylogenetic support/reference groups, uncertainty intervals, and a temporal panel corresponding to the headline H3 claim (R1-30). The reported compartment “variance shares” omit covariance between the compartment and expedition fitted components, so they should not be presented as a standard partition of explained variance (R1-31).

The README and manuscript should agree on the current surface contrast and spatial bootstrap interval; distinguish median-effect intervals from tests on site means (R1-32). Finally, Jordaan’s study is Australian, not Gobi, and predicted maximal growth potential should consistently be distinguished from observed growth in these soils (R1-33).
