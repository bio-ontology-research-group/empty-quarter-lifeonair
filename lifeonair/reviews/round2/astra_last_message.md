**Part 1 — Review**

The manuscript titled “Group 1l hydrogen scavengers are relatively depleted beside plant roots across the hyperarid Rub’ al-Khali” analyses 546 metagenomes from 59 sites using a 5,438-genome catalogue and read-based marker quantification. Its principal result is a root/bulk group-1l contrast of −2.275 log₂ across 106 visits at 47 sites, with an excess over reference markers of −1.827. New analyses relate this pattern to DNA concentration, genus composition and predicted growth potential, and examine surface and precipitation associations. The replicated, group-resolved relative root contrast remains the strongest contribution. The revision substantially improves the biological framing, but several new analyses contain implementation errors or support narrower conclusions than those presented.

I recommend **major revision**. The central relative-abundance observation survives independent recalculation. However, duplicated spatial records, incorrect handling of absent genera, overstated benchmark separation, incomplete contamination sensitivity and unsupported precipitation-robustness claims require correction. These are consequential problems in the revised evidence, not reasons to reopen the PI’s decisions about relative depletion, the narrowed root/surface comparison or exploratory framing.

I independently reaggregated all 1,716 usable sample-by-threshold records from the archived read counts: the maximum GE discrepancy was \(4.99994\times10^{-6}\), consistent with output rounding. All 573 screening metadata records specified 100-nt truncation and a 25-aa minimum alignment. ERA5 climatologies and antecedent sums also reproduced to numerical precision. Benchmark, genome-size and corrected-growth outputs matched their IBEX copies; the recorded jobs were 53613713, 53611804 and 53614016. I did not rerun DIAMOND, phylogenetic inference or the complete R workflows. The micromamba wrapper attempted temporary-file creation and was blocked by read-only permissions; subsequent recalculations used in-memory Python. No files were modified.

The PDF integrity scans detected no reviewer-directed instructions. Flags concerned small scientific labels, publisher marks such as `1234567890();,:`, and production headers. Figure typography remains a substantive presentation issue.

**Major issues**

**The spatial analysis contains duplicated observations, and its headline robustness claim contradicts its own sensitivity results (R2-01–02).** `topography.tsv` contains two records for site 19. The unrestricted merge in `h123_v2.R:345` duplicates that site throughout the later spatial calculations and duplicates its nine samples in the all-sample climate model. There are actually 51 bulk, 45 surface and 59 root sites, rather than the reported 52, 46 and 60. Using unique sites, I obtain bulk group-1l ERA5 \(\rho=-0.401\), versus the saved −0.373.

More seriously, “consistently across precipitation products and spatial sensitivity analyses” is false. In the saved surface group-1l results, NASA POWER gives \(\rho=-0.317\), \(q=0.0546\), and a six-block interval of −0.67 to +0.12. The blended product gives \(\rho=-0.207\), \(q=0.253\), with every block interval crossing zero. All four surface groups have nonsignificant blended-product correlations. The abstract and Discussion must distinguish the ERA5 result from cross-product robustness. Surface group-1l association also disappears after the implemented composition-axis adjustment; that adjustment uses a bulk-derived axis and should itself be labelled explicitly.

**The benchmark does not perform the exclusion described in the manuscript (R2-03).** Only EQ marker proteins enter clustering and fold assignment. HydDB and Cordero references remain in every database. Yet every retained group-1h protein, every retained group-2a protein and 997/1,111 retained group-1l proteins have a recorded HydDB hit at ≥50% identity. Thus the claim that “all proteins sharing 50% identity with the source” were removed is demonstrably incorrect. Clustering EQ sequences also does not itself establish an exhaustive identity boundary between folds. Report the existing experiment as EQ-cluster holdout with external references retained, and perform a genuinely sequence-disjoint benchmark if claiming performance on that basis. Distinguish reproducing computational labels from independently validating enzyme function.

**The benchmark exposes substantial CoxL error, while GE calibration remains untested (R2-04–05).** Under cluster holdout at the primary threshold, there are 14,594 true-form-I fragments assigned to form I, but also 4,285 other-CoxL fragments and 24 non-marker fragments assigned there. Precision is 0.772. This is not a negligible qualification to “the read classifier was specific for these groups.” Because other CoxL is much more abundant than form I, community-dependent cross-assignment could affect comparatively small CoxL contrasts. The required check is whether those contrasts survive a more discriminating classifier or an empirically bounded error model.

Separately, marker-fragment classification does not demonstrate that summed length-normalised hits divided by SCG signal recover copies per genome across these communities. SCGs are not held out or quantitatively benchmarked. Mock communities with known marker dosage and differing taxonomic composition should test the complete estimator. Until then, GE should be described as an estimated marker-to-SCG measure rather than an exactly calibrated genome count.

**Contamination sensitivity remains incomplete despite available controls and new results (R2-08–09).** The response’s claim that blanks exist only for expedition 5 is contradicted by the companion methods, which document six expedition-4 extraction blanks linked to 97 biological profiles. The current catalogue manuscript also documents a shotgun negative control. These controls should be assessed according to their assay and batch coverage.

Furthermore, none of the five representatives flagged by `decontam_batch.R` occurs in `reads/excl_v2_noflagged.txt`. The published contamination sensitivity therefore does not test the newly reported screen. Update the exclusion union and propagate it into the relevant read-based and MAG-based analyses, reporting the consequences separately from the broad genus-based flagging.

**The genus result remains an association requiring better uncertainty treatment, and community growth estimates can represent very little of a sample (R2-10–11).** The revised genus table still contains 121 exactly zero responses among 289 genera and 32 genera represented by one genome. Removing genera because their response equals zero is outcome-based selection, not a prevalence analysis. The site bootstrap added to the growth-shift intercept does not propagate uncertainty into the genus regression.

I reproduced the reported group-1l genus coefficient, −0.812, SE 0.180. An additional weighted regression with order fixed effects retained the association, approximately −1.035, SE 0.221. This diagnostic supports the association and argues against dismissing it solely as phylum confounding. It does not resolve catalogue ascertainment, incomplete-genome non-detection or dependence among genus responses estimated from the same sites.

Growth-prediction coverage is a separate problem. Although median coverage is approximately 97.7%, 26 primary samples have coverage below 80%; sample `55Sr3` has only 1.45%. Renormalising the predicted subset gives these samples an apparently complete community growth estimate. Report coverage by position and visit, repeat the growth-shift analysis after defensible coverage restrictions, and propagate uncertainty or bound the contribution of unpredicted genomes.

**The new aridity decomposition mishandles absent genera (R2-12).** `bsg <- aggregate(..., FUN=mean)` averages each genus only over visits represented in the sparse table. Missing genus/visit combinations are not zeros. Consequently, site-level genus weights sum to a mean of 1.209 and a maximum of 2.339. These are not community shares. Zero-filling before averaging restores sums of one and changes the observed and turnover-only correlations from −0.380/−0.367 to −0.430/−0.433. The qualitative interpretation may survive, but the decomposition and downstream mixed-genus analysis require regeneration from valid compositions.

**The temporal analysis is improved, but positive rainfall associations need stronger exposure and uncertainty checks (R2-15–17).** Extending ERA5 solves the missing-expedition problem. It does not establish temporal exposure accuracy. NASA POWER is used only for climatology, while the blended-product temporal sensitivity ends after expedition 4 and covers a different interval. A comparison on matched observations and an independent suitable precipitation or moisture product is needed before treating the surface/root 90-day signals as robust environmental responses.

Five shared expeditions also provide limited temporal replication. Fourteen of 35 repeatedly sampled bulk sites have only two visits, and only four have all five. The 60-site ERA5 record contains only 41 distinct daily series. Site resampling alone does not address shared weather errors or residual spatial dependence. Report leave-expedition-out stability, climate-grid/spatial sensitivity and bootstrap diagnostics. The current conditional associations can be informative without establishing general interannual responses.

The equivalence claims additionally concern `log2(GE + 0.01)`, not unmodified copies per genome. Group 2a is at or below that pseudocount in 176/546 samples. A ±0.5 bound on this transformed scale can conceal a materially larger proportional change near zero. Justify the biological bound and test its sensitivity to the transformation.

**The revised PERMANOVA still uses the wrong exchangeability unit (R2-18).** `how(blocks=site)` allows individual samples to exchange within a site across expeditions and positions. It does not preserve site-visit clusters. The primary dataset contains 60 position/visit cells with two replicates and 38 with three. A whole-visit permutation design, or an appropriately aggregated analysis preserving position structure, is required. The dispersion test also uses unrestricted permutations. Reporting dispersion is helpful, but does not repair the permutation design.

**Public reproducibility remains unfinished (R2-25).** The frozen tables make this audit possible, and the targeted IBEX provenance checks succeeded. Nevertheless, the advertised retrieval requires institutional credentials, upstream typing and some new input derivations are not supplied through a complete local entry point, and P3 claims remain outside the verification harness. The response’s assertion that all upstream scripts were copied is not borne out by `lifeonair/code/`. Supply the public archive, explicit dependencies, deterministic seeds and an executable P3 verification route before submission.

**Minor issues**

**Annotation reconciliation needs to be completed (R2-06–07).** The authoritative-looking `atmo_classification_v2.tsv` still labels 1,619 retained AYRGAGR entries as form I. The active analyses avoid this through separate corrected files, but downstream reuse is unsafe. Neighbourhood summaries also retain removed-contig loci: they count 1,124 group-1l proteins rather than the 1,111 retained proteins. Report consistent retained-locus denominators.

Absence of either searched CoxL motif is also not equivalent to a missing motif region. Two motif-less form-I calls are 790 and 810 residues long with >98% query coverage. Inspect the aligned active-site region, and distinguish truncation, sequence divergence and alternative function rather than treating all motif-less calls as truncated proteins.

**The DNA result should remain a conditional association (R2-13–14).** The reduction from −2.120 to −1.361 is a 35.8% attenuation of a regression coefficient, not a demonstrated biomass-mediated fraction. Nor does unequal behaviour among groups exclude compositional expansion: some guilds can increase alongside total biomass while others increase less. The baseline DNA model is useful, but the “single-kit visits” sensitivity drops kit adjustment across visits, and the bulk-only sensitivity omits the visit intercept. Preserve those distinctions and report cohort composition and diagnostics.

**Several new analyses need more precise estimand and method descriptions (R2-19–21).** The root decomposition’s −0.00465 is an abundance-weighted contribution to community carrier share, not a direct change in an average within-genus carrier fraction. Its turnover proportion is reproducible; my site bootstrap gives approximately 96.7–98.3%, conditional on the catalogue and decomposition convention. Describe an accounting decomposition rather than a demonstrated biological mechanism.

The mixed-genus precipitation model is a weighted Gaussian `lmer` on clipped logit fractions, not the binomial mixed model stated in Methods. Likewise, the CBB analysis is a three-KO co-occurrence screen. It does not validate RuBisCO form, catalytic competence, pathway completeness or missing-gene uncertainty. The reported 18%/8% should be labelled candidate co-carriage unless those checks are supplied.

**Multiplicity and null-result wording remain uneven (R2-22–23).** The code adjusts position tests separately by contrast and temporal tests separately by exposure; the manuscript’s broad family descriptions do not identify these boundaries. New CBB and mechanism tests also need a declared inferential status. Report all relevant estimates and intervals. Replace “changed no more than controls” with “no additional change was detected relative to controls,” unless a justified equivalence analysis is supplied. Raw root/bulk CoxL is significantly negative in the saved contrast table.

**The literature comparison needs one targeted addition and a more differentiated carbon interpretation (R2-24).** Li and López are now treated substantially more accurately. However, the new genome-trait section omits Wang et al. (2025), whose publisher abstract explicitly connects aridity, trace-gas use, genome size and predicted growth across 374 MAGs. The full text should be obtained for a focused comparison; I could verify the publisher abstract but not retrieve the complete article. [Wang et al., *Global Change Biology*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/gcb.70498)

The supplied Baril 2023 XML reports carbon-dependent responses of group-1h markers, and Kropp 2026 concerns glycerol-specific regulation of Huc, group 2a. These papers make carbon response more specific to substrate and physiological context; they should not be represented as evidence for a uniquely group-1l mechanism. Their digest summaries should not substitute for their actual findings.

**Figures and remaining wording need a final evidence-based pass (R2-26–28).** Figure 1 still contains text at about 4.1 pt in the compiled manuscript. Trees lack identifying tips and useful support annotation. Figure 2c combines different cohorts and estimands under “after adjustments”; label sample/site counts and interval methods for each row.

The assertion that controls “did not scale with DNA” conflicts with their estimated slopes and standard errors; their associations are smaller, not absent. The copy-number change is −0.185, rather than −0.20 to two decimals, and uses 105 paired visits. CoxL surface residual Moran’s test is \(p=0.048\), contradicting “but not for the other groups.” Qualify the remaining realised-growth wording.

Finally, 95.8% of the surface temperature predictor’s variance is explained by expedition. Its reported positive coefficient means that depletion weakens with higher temperature; it does not directly show heat-induced depletion. Describe the residual exposure contrast, its observational limitations and its stability before using it to motivate a surface-filter mechanism.
