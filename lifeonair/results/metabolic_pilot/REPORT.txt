Metabolic-model pilot: decision and evidence
12 October 2026

Decision

Do not add these simulations as mechanistic support for the paper's central
hypothesis, and do not expand to a catalogue-wide ecological regression yet.
The existing models are available at scale, but their hydrogenase representation
and reconstruction-dependent phenotypes are inadequate for that inference. This
is a limitation of the present models, not evidence that the organisms cannot
consume atmospheric hydrogen. The pilot does not currently justify stronger
claims or a higher-impact journal positioning.

Coverage and design

All 5,438 current representative IDs have gapsmith draft and filled models.
After quality, contamination-flag, subset and marker-group filters, 1,759 were
eligible: 178 group-1l carriers, 163 group-1h carriers and 1,418 neither controls.
We selected 24 triplets (72 MAGs) within 13 named orders, favouring genus/family
and quality matching, before inspecting phenotypes or field abundance. Selection
was deterministic and order-limited, not random or phylogenetically independent.
The controls lack group 1l/1h/2a and form-I CoxL; carriers exclude 1l/1h/2a
co-carriage but may contain form-I CoxL. The panel is a feasibility test.

Each model was evaluated as draft, filled, and filled with ungrounded H2 reactions
closed. The last variant retains an internal H2 reaction only if its GPR contains
an exact-sequence match to a curated group-1l/1h marker and the reaction was not
added by gap filling. It is a restrictive diagnostic, not a validated model repair.

We screened 12 organic substrates at 6 mmol organic C/gDW/h, and glucose at
0, 0.006, 0.06, 0.6 and 6 mmol C/gDW/h with optional H2 uptake capped at
0, 0.001, 0.01 and 0.1 mmol/gDW/h. The latter bounds are sensitivity settings;
they are not a conversion of atmospheric concentrations to fluxes. Oxygen was
capped at 10 and CO2 at 1 mmol/gDW/h. No exogenous organic donor was supplied in
the zero-organic-carbon condition. Biomass was closed for diagnostic ATP
hydrolysis, and restored for growth. Growth media additionally supplied trace
metals. Full bounds and substrate identities are in metabolic_simulate.py.

Primary results

All 10,584 scenario optimisations returned optimal status. All 216 model variants
passed the closed-exchange and inorganic-medium ATP checks. All 3,240 optional-H2
monotonicity checks passed. These controls do not constitute a complete mass or
charge audit: most SBML metabolites lack formulas.

Of the 48 marker carriers, 44 had exact matches between curated marker proteins
and the reconstruction input proteomes (21/24 group 1l; 23/24 group 1h). Only one
had a native internal H2 reaction whose GPR included that matched marker:
S54PRr1__self_SemiBin_621, group 1l. All model gene IDs were found in their input
proteomes; this is not explained by SBML gene-ID sanitisation. Four unmatched
carrier proteomes need separate annotation reconciliation before drawing an
absence conclusion.

No group-1l or group-1h model gained ATP from H2 at any tested carbon supply,
in any variant. One neither control, F21Dr3__single_SemiBin_84, gained about
0.15 mmol ATP/gDW/h in the filled model at H2 uptake 0.1; the benefit disappeared
when ungrounded H2 reactions were closed. Both the anoxic and all-H2-reaction
knockout controls also yielded zero ATP (tolerance 1e-6) in all 216 variants.
A neither label excludes the named
high-affinity marker groups, not every possible hydrogen-metabolising enzyme.

Median numbers of organic substrates supporting ATP production (out of 12):
                          group 1l       group 1h       neither
Draft                         3              5             4
Filled                        6              6.5           6
Filled, H2 restricted         6              6.5           6

Within the 24 matched triplets, filled-model breadth differences (1l minus
control) had median -1 against 1h and 0 against neither. Against 1h, 13 were
negative, 5 tied and 6 positive; against neither, 10 were negative, 5 tied and
9 positive. These descriptive results do not establish a distinct resource-use
strategy. They measure feasibility of ATP production under the chosen media,
not experimentally measured substrate use or growth.

None of the 72 drafts grew even in the permissive all-exchange positive control.
All 72 filled models did. None grew on any of the 12 individually supplied
substrates in the defined mineral growth medium, in any variant. Therefore zero
minimal-medium growth cannot be interpreted as substrate non-utilisation.
Closing ungrounded H2 reactions also abolished permissive growth in four filled
models (one 1l and three 1h); this illustrates the broader network consequences
of the restrictive diagnostic.

Independent reconstruction sensitivity

Of 26 single-sample pilot MAGs, 25 had gapseq models whose original source genomes
matched current representatives by exact unordered contig-sequence hashes. One
lacked the required source/model. We ran 750 additional ATP optimisations on
these 25 draft/filled pairs; all were optimal and all closed/inorganic checks
passed. The subset comprises 11 group-1l, 7 group-1h and 7 neither MAGs and is
not the balanced primary panel.

Five filled gapseq models gained ATP from H2: one group 1l, three group 1h and
one neither. Two were already positive in drafts. None was the gapsmith-positive
MAG, so 6/25 paired filled reconstructions disagreed on the binary H2 phenotype.
Filled gapseq median carbon ATP breadth was 10/10/9, versus 6/8/8 in gapsmith
for those same group-1l/group-1h/neither genomes. These differences show material
sensitivity to reconstruction, not a robust group-1l-specific phenotype.

Translation of the coordinate-named gapseq H2-reaction GPR genes yielded no exact
matches to the curated hydrogenase sequences (122 gene/reaction/variant rows).
This conservative mapping does not exclude homologous genes with different
predicted boundaries; it does not validate enzyme specificity, affinity, electron
acceptors or energy coupling. The positive gapseq results remain hypotheses
about modelled capacity, not evidence of atmospheric hydrogen oxidation.

Implication and next useful work

The defensible next step is focused curation of several matched lineages, not
more automated FBA across thousands of the same uncurated reconstructions.
Reconcile marker coordinates and alternative start sites; inspect catalytic and
small subunits, maturation genes and electron-transfer partners; then establish
reaction direction, cofactors, membrane coupling and transport from independent
biochemical evidence. Audit mass/charge balance, flux cycles, biomass demands
and reconstruction-specific media. Keep draft/gap-fill provenance explicit and
do not add reactions merely to obtain the desired hydrogen phenotype.

Once that produces defensible positive controls, test energy maintenance and
carbon sparing across uptake assumptions, before fitting model traits to the
paired root/bulk data. Shared flux constraints alone cannot establish affinity
or group-1l versus group-1h performance at atmospheric concentrations. Direct
H2-consumption measurements or another independent functional validation would
be needed to turn a model-based hypothesis into strong physiological evidence.

The central paper and its conclusions were not revised on the basis of this
pilot. The planned field regression was conditional on model validity and is
not scientifically justified by the present outputs.
