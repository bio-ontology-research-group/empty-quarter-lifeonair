SYNTHESIS:
This literature indicates that claims regarding the failure of space-for-time substitution (H3) and the decoupling of H2 and CO capacities (H2) must be framed carefully to avoid claiming novelty for established ecological dynamics:

1. **Space-for-Time Failure is Mechanistically Expected (H3):** Lovell (2023), Demergasso (2023), and Imminger (2024) establish that hyperarid soil microbiomes prioritize basal repair and metabolic maintenance without rapid cell division or population turnover during ephemeral wetting pulses. Do not overstate the temporal decoupling from antecedent rainfall as a paradox; frame it as physiological inertia where spatial gradients reflect millennial evolutionary sorting, whereas temporal pulses reflect non-growth survival.
2. **Selective Depletion of Group 1l vs Retention of 1h/CoxL (H1):** While Marasco (2022) confirms intense biotic competition in hyperarid rhizosheaths and Hou (2025) outlines oligotrophic traits of high-affinity HOB, Dip (2025) challenges sweeping root repression. Frame the 4.7-fold depletion of group 1l as displacement of narrow obligate scavengers by competitive copiotrophs, whereas retained group 1h and form-I CoxL reflect facultative mixotrophic plasticity (Islam 2019).
3. **Capacity vs Flux (Limitations):** Buzzard (2022), León-Sobrino (2019), and Schulze-Makuch (2018) prove that transient moisture decouples in situ gas consumption from background gene abundance, validating our strict emphasis on genomic capacity rather than flux.
