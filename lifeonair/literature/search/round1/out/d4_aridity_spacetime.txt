SYNTHESIS:
This literature provides critical boundary conditions and highlights claims to nuance in the manuscript:
1. **Space-for-time failure is predictable, not anomalous**: Framing the breakdown of space-for-time substitution as an unexpected discovery would be overstated. Macroecological theory (Blois 2013; Lovell 2023) predicts that spatial gradients fail to predict temporal responses whenever temporal climate variance during observation is small relative to the geographic gradient. In the Rub' al-Khali, episodic pulses of a few millimeters cannot replicate a 34 mm MAP gradient established over millennia.
2. **Decadal legacies govern genomic capacity**: Work by Hawkes (2017), Canarini (2021), and Broderick (2025) demonstrates that multi-decadal precipitation history imprints persistent "functional legacies" and "ecological memory." Group 1l hydrogenase capacity reflects deep evolutionary trait filtering (Malik 2020), whereas antecedent rain pulses trigger only brief transcriptional bursts (León-Sobrino 2024) or lethal osmotic shocks (Azua-Bustos 2018) without restructuring the baseline metagenome.
3. **Reconciling contrasting findings**: Contrary to our observed multi-year inertia (expedition explaining 2% variance), studies in other drylands report significant community restructuring after drought or rain (Uritskiy 2019; Ochoa-Hueso 2018; de Vries 2018). The manuscript must explicitly emphasize that shotgun metagenomic capacity measures static, constitutive genetic potential rather than dynamic physiological fluxes.
