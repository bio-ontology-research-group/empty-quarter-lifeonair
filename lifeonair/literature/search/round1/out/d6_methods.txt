SYNTHESIS:
This literature surfaces three critical vulnerabilities for the manuscript:

1. **Compositional vs. Absolute Depletion (H1):** [Azarbad et al. (2022)](file:///10.3389/fmicb.2021.798023) demonstrated that relative profiling in rhizosphere soils routinely generates spurious depletion artifacts for oligotrophs when total microbial load expands. Even normalised to genome equivalents (copies/cell), a 4.7-fold per-genome drop in group 1l might reflect dilution by expanding rhizosphere taxa rather than a net per-gram loss of scavenging capacity. The manuscript must explicitly frame this limitation or confirm total cell loads.

2. **Growth Rate Attribution (H1):** The conclusion that growth rate/copiotrophy explains only a minor part of root shifts conflicts with [López et al. (2023)](file:///10.3389/s41396-023-01453-6), who found maximal growth rate is the primary driver of rhizosphere enrichment. However, [Weissman et al. (2022)](file:///10.1128/msystems.00745-22) showed gRodon metagenome mode suffers strong GC bias, which may dilute true ecological associations in GC-skewed desert actinobacteria.

3. **Group 1l Ecological Niches (H1/H3):** [Imminger et al. (2024)](file:///10.1038/s41467-024-46920-6) showed that desert biocrust 1l hydrogenases are transcriptionally induced during hydration rather than desiccation. Assuming 1l is solely a starvation scavenger overstates its functional role.

Finally, meeting [Fierer et al. (2025)](file:///10.1038/s41564-025-02035-2) contamination reporting standards is mandatory for hyperarid metagenomes at *ISME Journal*.
