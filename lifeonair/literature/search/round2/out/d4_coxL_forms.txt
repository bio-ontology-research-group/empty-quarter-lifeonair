SYNTHESIS:
Aerobic CO dehydrogenase large subunits ([`CoxL`](file:///home/leechuck/Documents/papers/empty-quarter-mags/lifeonair/literature/search)) bifurcate into Form I (canonical, group OMP) and Form II (divergent, group BMS). Form I is biochemically defined by the active-site motif AYXCSFR (and close variants like AYACSFR, SYRCSFR), where an invariant cysteine coordinates Cu in the binuclear [Cu-S-Mo] cluster via dedicated CoxDEF chaperones. Culturing studies (e.g., Roseobacter, forest soils) consistently demonstrate that only Form I confers high-affinity atmospheric CO scavenging; Form II (AYRGAGR), which substitutes cysteine for glycine and typically lacks *coxDEF*, fails to oxidize CO in these models. However, experimental evidence from *Bradyrhizobium japonicum*, *Kyrpidia spormannii*, and *Aeropyrum pernix* proves that select organisms harboring only Form II can oxidize CO, likely at higher concentrations. Restricting Rub' al-Khali profiling to Form I accurately isolates atmospheric scavengers, but the manuscript should explicitly frame Form II omission as a methodological limitation.
