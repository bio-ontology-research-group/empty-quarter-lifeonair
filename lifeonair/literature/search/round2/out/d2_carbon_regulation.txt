SYNTHESIS:
High-affinity [NiFe]-hydrogenase (groups 1h, 1l, 2a) and Form I CoxL regulation provides a biochemical explanation for the depletion of group 1l scavengers in root-adjacent hyperarid soils. Pure-culture and microcosm studies establish that group 1h enzymes (Hhy) and CoxL are induced under severe organic carbon starvation for maintenance energy, whereas labile carbon (glycerol, sucrose) triggers catabolite repression via regulators like GylR and depletes high-affinity uptake genes ([Baril 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/femsec/fiad159), [Kropp 2026](https://doi.org/10.1128/msystems.01678-25)). This supports our interpretation that root exudation relieves energy starvation, rendering group 1l scavenging non-essential and disadvantageous relative to fast-growing copiotrophs. However, findings of simultaneous trace-gas co-oxidation supporting growth ([Tveit 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/microorganisms9010153)) and group 2a expression during exponential phases ([Cordero 2019](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.RA119.011076)) challenge a purely starvation-centric model. Rather than being universally repressed, trace gas oxidizers exhibit divergent niches: slow-growing group 1l persistence specialists are depleted beside roots, whereas mixotrophs may exploit both niches.
