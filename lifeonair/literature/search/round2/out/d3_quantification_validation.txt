SYNTHESIS:
Quantifying functional capacity from short-read metagenomes requires distinguishing biological depletion from computational and sequencing artifacts. Normalisation frameworks (MicrobeCensus, MUSiCC, GCompip, SingleM) demonstrate that dividing target gene coverages by universal single-copy ribosomal protein equivalents reliably controls for library depth and community genome-size variation. Furthermore, DIAMOND-based competitive searches scale robustly across complex metagenomes. However, two methodological challenges threaten read-based inferences: first, uniform similarity thresholds produce substantial false-positive matches among paralogous oxidoreductases sharing conserved motifs (Carr 2014, Orellana 2017, Tamames 2019), necessitating competitive database architectures and active-site motif verification. Second, Illumina sequencing exhibits systematic GC-dependent coverage bias (Browne 2020), which could artificially distort read recovery for higher-GC group 1l carriers. Defending our Rub' al-Khali results in *ISME Journal* requires coupling our held-out classifier benchmark with explicit evaluations of motif-level precision and GC-bias sensitivity analyses.
