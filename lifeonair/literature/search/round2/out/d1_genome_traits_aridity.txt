SYNTHESIS:
Across global drylands and environmental gradients, genomic traits reflect sharp trade-offs between metabolic streamlining and physiological versatility. Broad macroecological surveys (Liu 2023, Simonsen 2022) suggest aridity enforces genome reduction to conserve energy. However, our findings reveal a more nuanced divergence in Rub' al-Khali soils: while high-affinity group 1l scavengers exhibit streamlined genome sizes and elevated GC content driven by carbon limitation and DNA stability (Chuckran 2023), groups 1h, 2a, and form-I CoxL retain larger genomes to preserve mixotrophic and regulatory breadth. Slower predicted maximum growth rates across all carriers reflect conservative oligotrophic strategies adapted to chronic starvation (Dragone 2022, Roller 2016). Furthermore, the relative depletion of group 1l scavengers adjacent to plant roots aligns with carbon catabolite repression triggered by labile root exudates (Kropp 2026), shifting local communities toward fast-growing copiotrophs and demonstrating that trace-gas oxidation capacity is governed by microhabitat resource partitioning.
