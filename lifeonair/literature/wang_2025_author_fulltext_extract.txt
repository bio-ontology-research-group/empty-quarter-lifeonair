L109: ABSTRACT
L110: Microbes acquire energy to sustain their survival and function through the decomposition of organic carbon (C) or by oxidizing
L111:
L112: atmospheric trace gases (e.g., H 2, CO, CH 4). However, how these two microbial energy- acquisition strategies change along en-
L113:
L114: vironmental gradients and the underlying mechanisms are unclear. This study investigated the energy strategies and genomic
L115:
L116: traits of soil microbiomes along a natural aridity gradient, ranging from semi- humid forests to arid deserts. By analyzing 374
L117: metagenome- assembled genomes from 13 microbial phyla, we found that the most prevalent microbes are metabolically versatile
L118:
L119: aerobes that use atmospheric trace gases to support aerobic respiration, C fixation, and N, P, and S cycling. Soil microbes adapt
L120:
L121: genomic traits associated with reduced energy expenditure in more arid soils, including smaller genome sizes, lower GC content,
L122: and fewer 16S rRNA gene copies. Microbial communities in diverse arid habitats are capable of utilizing organic compounds and
L123:
L124: the oxidation of trace gases (e.g., H 2, CO, CH 4, and H 2 S) as energy sources. However, the utilization of organic energy decreased
L125:
L126: while reliance on trace gas oxidation increased with increasing aridity. Higher consumption rates of H 2, CO, and CH 4 in desert
L127: soils from ex situ culture experiments confirmed that increased aridity stimulates microbial oxidation of atmospheric trace gases.
L128:
L129: This shift in energy utilization was strongly correlated with declining soil organic C levels. As organic C decreased along the
L130:
L131: aridity gradient, the abundance of trace gas oxidizers (both specialized and multi- gas oxidizers) increased significantly, while
L132: that of non- oxidizers declined. Trace gas oxidizers exhibited smaller genomes, lower 16S rRNA operon copy numbers, and slower
L133:
L134: predicted growth rates, indicative of oligotrophic lifestyles. In contrast, copiotrophic non- oxidizers had larger genomes and faster
L135:
L136: growth rates. These findings reveal that microbial communities adapt their genomic traits and energy- acquisition strategies to
L137: sustain functionality across aridity gradients, enhancing our understanding of soil microbiome responses to climate change.

L139: 1 | Introduction
L140:
L141: Aridification is one of the most pressing global challenges of this
L142:
L143: century and threatens terrestrial biodiversity, food security, and
L144:
L145: ecosystem functions (Tariq etal.2024). In the context of climate
L146:
L147: change, elevated temperatu re, abnormal precipitation, increased
L148:
L149: evaporation, and groundwater depletion have contributed to the
L150: expansion of arid regions at a rate of 14.3 × 10 4 km 2 over the past
L151:
L152: three decades (UN2024). By 2050, more than 70% of arable land
L153:
L154: is expected to face the impacts of severe drought (IPCC 2022).
L155:
L156: Drought- induced water scarcity and reduced carbon (C) avail-
L157:
L158: ability jeopardize the survival of soil microbial communities

L163:
L164: and impair their ecological functions (Wang et al. 2014; Hu
L165: etal.2021; Kong etal.2022). Understanding the survival strat-
L166:
L167: egies of microbiomes under drought stress is therefore critical
L168:
L169: for predicting and mitigating the impacts of climate change on
L170:
L171: terrestrial ecosystems.
L172:
L173: Genomic trait variation is a fundamental mechanism of mi-
L174:
L175: crobial adaptation to environmental stress. Key traits such as
L176:
L177: average genome size, GC content, and rRNA gene copy num-
L178:
L179: bers have been established as critical indicators for capturing
L180: microbial life history strategies and resource allocation patterns
L181:
L182: (Gallet etal. 2017; Pereira- Flores et al. 2019; Luan etal.2020).
L183:
L184: Although drought stress has been shown to have profound ef-
L185:
L186: fects on genomic traits, its specific effects remain controver-
L187:
L188: sial. Early evidence from organismal metabolism suggests that
L189:
L190: elevated temperatures enhance the reproduction rates and
L191:
L192: metabolic processes of microbial communities. Consequently,
L193:
L194: Copiotrophs whose genomes encode more ribosomal rRNA
L195: genes supporting rapid growth become resource- dependent to
L196:
L197: prioritize reproduction and rapidly occupy ecological niches
L198:
L199: (Roller etal. 2016). As predicted by the Red Queen Hypothesis
L200:
L201: (RQH), microbial communities increase their functional com-
L202:
L203: plexity through genomic expansion, proposing that microbes
L204:
L205: invest in diverse resource acquisition strategies to accelerate
L206:
L207: growth, which is favorable for survival and colonization in chal-
L208:
L209: lenging environments (Hall and Scanlan 2011). By contrast,
L210: some researchers have found that drought and associated envi-
L211:
L212: ronmental changes, such as water limitation, impose selection
L213:
L214: pressures on microbial communities (Banerjee etal.2019; Ullah
L215:
L216: etal.2018). Oligotrophs, which are more competitive at the ex-
L217:
L218: pense of growth due to their higher resource- use efficiency, are
L219:
L220: linked to energy- efficient strategies (Chen and Kushwaha2021).
L221:
L222: This aligns with the evolutionary framework of the Black
L223:
L224: Queen Hypothesis (BQH), which emphasizes microbial genome
L225: streamlining to enhance resource- use efficiency and reduce en-
L226:
L227: ergy costs (Giovannoni etal. 2014). Similarly, drought restricts
L228:
L229: microbial energy availability by reducing plant productivity and
L230:
L231: organic C content, which can also drive genome streamlining
L232:
L233: (Flagg etal.2023). Therefore, in contrast to the RQH, microbial
L234:
L235: genome size may decrease in increasingly arid environments,
L236:
L237: with oligotrophs becoming more prevalent.
L238:
L239: Cyanobacteria and microalgae are traditionally regarded as pri-
L240: mary producers in soils—capable of generating organic C to sup-
L241:
L242: port organoheterotrophic bacteria (Cary etal. 2010). However,
L243:
L244: evidence from deserts and arid ecosystems indicates that these
L245:
L246: photoautotrophic producers are extremely scarce, limiting their
L247:
L248: ability to supply sufficient energy to the microbial community
L249:
L250: (Pointing and Belnap 2012; Bay, Waite, and Hugenholtz2021).
L251:
L252: Growing evidence indicates that certain soil microbial mem-
L253:
L254: bers can scavenge atmospheric trace gases (H 2 and CO) for sur-
L255: vival or as alternative energy sources. For example, in volcanic
L256:
L257: craters (Weber and King 2010; Greening etal.2015), Antarctic
L258:
L259: dry valleys (Cary etal.2010), and arid deserts (Ji etal.2 017; Li
L260:
L261: etal.2023), these gases are considered dominant energy sources
L262:
L263: that microbes highly rely on. This unconventional energy ac-
L264:
L265: quisition strategy challenges the conventional view that soil
L266:
L267: microbial communities in arid ecosystems primarily rely on
L268: phototrophy (Bay, Waite, and Hugenholtz2021). Although nu-
L269:
L270: merous studies have highlighted the critical role of trace gas
L271:
L272: oxidation in sustaining microbial energy demands, such reports
L273:
L274: have predominantly focused on regional scales and limited
L275:
L276: metagenome- assembled genomes (MAGs, typically < 100), with
L277:
L278: few addressing life history strategies of microbes utilizing these
L279:
L280: distinct energy sources. A recent study from arid ecosystems
L281:
L282: has shown that the oxidation rates of trace gases increase along
L283: an aridity gradient and correlate with reduced organic C (Li
L284:
L285: etal.2023). In more arid, C- limited habitats, trace gas- oxidizing
L286:
L287: microbes may confer a competitive advantage. Similarly, or-
L288:
L289: ganic C has been demonstrated to influence microbial life strat-
L290:
L291: egies (Leung etal.2020), as oligotrophs commonly inhabit these
L292:
L293: nutrient- poor environments and are more likely to utilize trace
L294:
L295: gases as an energy source, thereby sustaining local ecosystem
L296: functions (Greening and Grinter 2022). Therefore, integrating
L297:
L298: trace gas oxidation capacity with survival strategies of oligotro-
L299:
L300: phic microbes when exploring aridity gradients is scientifically
L301:
L302: justified.
L303:
L304: Here, we assessed how soil microbes modulate their genomic
L305:
L306: traits and energy utilization in response to increasing aridity.
L307:
L308: We hypothesize that: (i) consistent with a strategy of genome
L309:
L310: streamlining, soil microbes in arid environments exhibit ge-
L311: nomic traits associated with low energy investment, including
L312:
L313: smaller genome sizes, lower GC content, and fewer 16S rRNA
L314:
L315: gene copies; (ii) as aridity increases, microbes intensify their re-
L316:
L317: liance on atmospheric trace gases (e.g., H 2, CO, CH 4, and H 2 S)
L318:
L319: to maintain soil functions in more arid habitats; and (iii) shifts
L320:
L321: in energy utilization strategies are closely linked to changes
L322:
L323: in microbial genomic traits and life history strategies. To test
L324: these hypotheses, soil samples were collected along a natural
L325:
L326: aridity gradient spanning semi- humid forests, semi- arid grass-
L327:
L328: lands, and arid deserts. Binning- based metagenomic shotgun
L329:
L330: sequencing and energy utilization analyses were performed (Li
L331:
L332: etal.2023; Bay, Dong, etal.2021). Our study elucidates micro-
L333:
L334: bial survival mechanisms along an aridity gradient, providing
L335:
L336: a foundation for understanding soil microbiome responses to
L337:
L338: climate change.
